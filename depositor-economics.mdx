Depositors are the supply side. They earn from the pool for as long as their position is live, and the size of their backing is the one strategic decision they make.

## Income streams

| Stream | Who earns it | Mechanic |
|---|---|---|
| Ticket share | Every live position, equally | 94% of every ticket, split equally per position. A $25 common earns the same base share as an $8,000 grail |
| Crown tithe | The five top-backed positions | 5% of every ticket, split 40/25/15/12/8 across the board's ranks. See [The crown](/the-crown) |
| Exit proceeds | A position that gets kept | The backing returned, minus a 1% settlement cut |

The equal split rewards *being in the pool*; the crown board rewards *size*. Together they keep the long tail of commons listed while still bribing grails in, and neither needs a token.

Fee income accrues onchain and is claimable at any time, paid straight to your USDC account. Gabox's own pools charge no performance fee on it.

## The backing decision

Choosing `B` is the core strategy, and it replaced vault risk as the thing a depositor reasons about:

| | Back low | Back high |
|---|---|---|
| Draw frequency | Drawn fast | Rarely drawn |
| Capital | Freed quickly | Locked a long time |
| Fee income | The same equal split as everyone, over a short tenure | The same equal split, over a long tenure |
| Crown board | Out of reach | Possibly a rank, up to 40% of the tithe |
| Danger | Item pulled cheap, you underpriced it | Low yield on locked capital |

There is an optimum between the extremes, and the deposit flow surfaces it: when a depositor sets a backing, the UI shows in real time the implied draw odds, expected tenure, projected fee APY, and whether the amount takes a board rank. Nobody should misprice blindly. The minimum backing is $25.

## The buffer: prepaid re-listing

A depositor can post a **buffer** alongside the backing: a prepaid reserve in the same units. When a buyer draws the item and sells it back, the sell-back is paid from the backing as usual, and the buffer automatically refills the position, which stays live with the same weight and uninterrupted fee accrual. `$100 backing + $400 buffer` is five lives.

The buffer is not a discount and costs exactly what re-depositing would: it is automatic re-listing that saves the depositor from noticing, paying gas, and re-listing a card that will be rejected a dozen times before someone wants it.

## What risk means here

**Risk is opportunity cost, not principal loss.** A rational depositor backs at the asset's honest value, so whichever way a draw goes they are whole:

- **Kept**: they receive their backing back, minus the 1% settlement cut, plus accrued fees.
- **Sold back**: they pay the backing (that is the buyback they signed up for), and they hold the card again, or the buffer relists it automatically and the fees keep accruing.
- **Never drawn**: they can withdraw at any time, unless the position is mid-draw at that moment.

The only true downside is a low return: being drawn and kept early, before much fee income accrued, returns the backing with little on top. There is no lockup and this trade-off is disclosed up front.

<Warning>
The exception is deliberate underbacking. Back a $500 card at $50 and someone will draw it and keep it for $50. The mechanism does not protect a depositor from underpricing their own item; that cost falling on the mispricer is what keeps pool pricing honest.
</Warning>
