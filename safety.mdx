The design's headline safety property is what it removes: there is no protocol vault, no float, and no oracle, so the two failure modes that dominate vault-based designs cannot occur at all.

## Eliminated by construction

| Risk | Why it cannot happen |
|---|---|
| Risk-of-ruin (house insolvency) | Every buyback is the depositor's own escrowed backing. The protocol holds no float, so there is nothing to drain |
| Oracle manipulation | No floor oracle exists in the price path. Pricing derives only from collateralized, self-set backings |
| Draw manipulation | Every draw runs against a snapshot of the pool frozen at payment, with its price and rates stamped; MagicBlock VRF makes the randomness verifiable. Neither deposit timing, withdrawal timing, nor a parameter change can alter a draw already bought |
| Retroactive repricing | Every rate a buyer or depositor is exposed to is stamped on their draw or position when they pay, so no admin parameter change can touch money already committed |

## Managed risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Underbacking snipe: back a good item too low, buyers pull it cheap | Self-correcting, the loss lands on the mispricing depositor; asset illiquidity blunts instant arbitrage; whitelisting and the $25 backing floor bound what enters |
| Crown-board games: flash capital taking a rank for a single pull | The commitment window: a rank earns only after being held through 32 fee recognitions, so rank costs committed capital. A whale camping rank 1 is structurally capped at 40% of the tithe. See [The crown](/the-crown) |
| Wash trading and self-dealing | Base economics self-limit it: pulling your own position still pays the ticket, and 94% of it goes to the whole pool. Only dangerous if token emissions ever reward volume, in which case: per-wallet position and volume caps, sybil resistance |
| Regulatory (gambling) | Geo-blocking from day one; verifiable VRF odds published per pool; cards framing, since physical booster packs are a century-old, well-understood randomized product |
| Oracle outage | A draw that gets no answer can be re-armed by anyone after ~2 minutes and abandoned for a full ticket refund after a day. The buyer's money cannot be stranded behind a silent oracle |

## How the program is verified

The Solana program is complete and live on devnet, where it has settled real draws against the real MagicBlock VRF oracle. Beyond conventional unit tests, the suite:

- checks **19 pool-wide invariants** (conservation, solvency, per-party credit ledgers) after generated sequences of instructions, compared against an independent reference model of the design,
- verifies its own tests by **mutation**: known defects are reintroduced and must be caught by a named assertion, and
- has been through an internal security review that specifically hunted fund-loss paths; everything found was fixed and pinned with regression tests before any mainnet plan.

The upgrade authority moves to a Squads multisig before mainnet, and it is the trust root worth scrutinizing: the program is upgradeable, so the multisig, not the code, is the final safety boundary. It will be published.

## Why fungible tokens are excluded

Scope is a safety feature. A liquid token has a hard, known DEX price, so an underbacked token position would be arbitraged mechanically and the pool drained. NFTs and tokenized assets have fuzzy floors, which makes self-set backing self-correcting instead of exploitable. See [Overview](/#scope).

## The buyer's real risk

None of this makes pulling tickets a good investment. The ticket costs EV plus a 10% surcharge, and a sell-back returns 90% of the drawn item's backing, so the expected value of a pull is negative, and that is disclosed. What the design guarantees is narrower and honest: the odds are verifiable, the prize is real, and the instant sell-back will always pay, because the money is already there.
