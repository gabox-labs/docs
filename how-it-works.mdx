Gabox connects two sides of a market through a pool. Depositors supply assets they are willing to part with at a price. Buyers pay a flat ticket for a random shot at everything inside. The protocol sits in the middle, taking a small cut and holding no risk of its own.

## The loop

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[Depositor lists asset<br/>+ posts backing B] --> B[Position joins pool<br/>weight = 1 / B]
    B --> C[Buyer pays ticket<br/>EV x 1.10]
    C --> D[VRF selects one position<br/>card delivered]
    D --> E{Buyer decides}
    E -->|Keep| F[Asset stays with buyer<br/>Backing returned to depositor<br/>Depositor exits]
    E -->|Sell back| G[Buyer gets 90% of B in cash<br/>Asset back to depositor<br/>or buffer relists it]
    G --> B
```

## Step by step

<Steps>
  <Step title="Deposit">
    A depositor lists an NFT or tokenized asset and pairs it with **backing**, an amount of USDC ($25 minimum) they lock next to the asset. Backing is the depositor's own statement of the item's value: it is the price they are willing to buy it back at. They can add a **buffer** on top, a prepaid reserve that relists the item automatically after sell-backs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pricing">
    The pool's ticket price updates automatically. It equals the draw-weighted expected value of the pool (the harmonic mean of all backings) plus a 10% surcharge. See [Pricing](/pricing).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Draw">
    A buyer pays the ticket. MagicBlock VRF selects exactly one position, with probability inversely proportional to its backing: cheap items are drawn constantly, richly backed grails rarely. Each draw runs against a snapshot of the pool frozen at payment, so nothing can interfere with a request in flight. The card is delivered to the buyer immediately. See [The draw](/the-draw).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep or sell back">
    Holding the card, the buyer has about five minutes to choose. **Keep** it (also the timeout default), in which case the depositor gets their backing returned and exits. Or **sell it back** for 90% of its backing in instant cash, in which case the card returns to its depositor, or the buffer relists it and it keeps earning. See [Keep or sell back](/keep-or-sell-back).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The three roles

| Role | What they put in | What they get out | What they risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Depositor | Asset + backing capital | An equal share of 94% of every ticket, backing returned on exit, possibly a crown-board rank | Opportunity cost only: being drawn early before fees accrue |
| Buyer | Ticket price | A random item worth keeping, or instant cash at 90% of its backing | The spread between the ticket and what a pull returns |
| Protocol | Nothing (no float, no vault) | 1% of each ticket, plus 10% of the backing on each sell-back | Nothing: it never owes anyone anything |

## Why the house cannot go broke

There is no house bankroll. Every buyback a buyer might claim is the depositor's own backing, locked before the item entered the pool. The protocol never promises a payout it does not already hold in escrow on someone's behalf. Solvency is structural, not a treasury management problem.

<Info>
This is the core difference from vault-based designs, where a lucky streak of jackpot cash-outs can drain a shared buyback fund. In Gabox that scenario cannot exist. See [Design evolution](/design-evolution).
</Info>
